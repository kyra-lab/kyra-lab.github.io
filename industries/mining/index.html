Mine water and metals recovery

Mine water is variable, often full of solids and dominated by different salts from one site to the next. Those are the conditions where conventional membranes struggle and where an adaptive system is useful.

  • Mine water for reuse
  • Salt control in bioleaching

The challenge

Active and closed mines pump vast volumes of water that must be treated before release. At the same time, metal recovery processes like bioleaching are limited by salt they cannot easily remove.

  • Treat-and-discharge costs
    Large pumped volumes, treated in perpetuity at closed sites, with nothing recovered.
  • Every site is different
    Some waters are chloride-dominated, others sulphate-dominated from pyrite oxidation. One fixed design does not fit all.
  • Solids and scaling
    Reverse osmosis is easily fouled by solids and scaling minerals, which mine water carries in quantity.
  • Salt in leach circuits
    In bioleaching, salt builds up in the recirculating solution and has to be controlled without losing copper or iron.

Progress so far

  • Water-chemistry modelling across chloride-dominated, sulphate-dominated and mixed mine waters
  • Best suited to waters up to about 10,000 µS/cm, with a sweet spot below 5,000 µS/cm
  • In discussion with a copper bioleaching company on salt control

Mine water for reuse

Feasibility

Treatment of pumped mine water, after conventional settlement, to a quality that can be used for agriculture or industry rather than simply discharged.

What it changes

  • Turns a perpetual treatment cost into a water resource
  • Tolerates the variability between and within sites
  • Handles conditions that foul reverse osmosis

Best suited to

Mine water schemes with moderate salinity, particularly sulphate-dominated and mixed waters.

Where it stands

Modelling across site types, including lime and caustic pre-treatment

Salt control in bioleaching

Exploring

Selective removal of sodium chloride from bioleach solutions, keeping copper and iron in the circuit so the iron can be reused by the bacteria.

What it changes

  • Keeps salinity where the process needs it
  • Retains valuable copper and reusable iron
  • Reduces bleed and make-up volumes

Best suited to

Heap and tank bioleaching of low and medium-grade copper ores, such as chalcopyrite.

Where it stands

Technical discussions with an industry partner
