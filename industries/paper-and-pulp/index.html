Water reuse for paper and pulp mills

Mills are being pushed to use less fresh water. The barrier to closing loops further is salt: chlorides, sulphates and calcium build up until they cause scaling, corrosion and quality problems. Danu Water removes them, so the water can keep circulating.

  • Recycle loop salt control
  • Treated effluent to fresh water
  • Concentrate as a resource

The challenge

Paper is one of the most water-intensive industries, and many mills sit in regions where water is getting scarcer and discharge limits are tightening.

  • Salt build-up in closed loops
    Every time water is recirculated, dissolved salts concentrate. Above a threshold they cause deposits, scaling, corrosion and runnability problems.
  • Rising freshwater and discharge costs
    Abstraction licences, water tariffs and effluent charges are all moving in one direction.
  • Water stress
    In drought-prone regions, water restrictions can force mills to cut production.
  • Variable water
    Mill water changes with grade, furnish and season. Fixed treatment systems struggle to keep up.

Progress so far

  • Industrial pilot unit operating at WEPA's Bridgend mill, Wales, since October 2026
  • Seven-week pilot programme at Essity's Valls site, Spain, from November 2026
  • Letters of intent from WEPA and Essity

What we offer

3 offerings for paper & pulp. Each status shows how far it has got.

Recycle loop salt control

Industrial pilot

A side-stream on the white water or process water loop that removes dissolved salts continuously, so the loop stays below the level where problems start.

What it changes

  • Close loops further and cut freshwater intake
  • Less scaling, corrosion and deposit-related downtime
  • Steadier water chemistry for the paper machine

Best suited to

Mills running tight water circuits, or planning to, where conductivity or hardness is the limit.

Where it stands

Industrial pilot at WEPA Bridgend; testing at Essity Valls from November 2026

Treated effluent to fresh water

Industrial pilot

Polishing of biologically treated effluent to a quality that can replace part of the mill's freshwater intake, instead of sending it to discharge.

What it changes

  • A water source the mill controls, independent of drought restrictions
  • Smaller discharge volumes and lower effluent charges
  • A step towards near-closed water use

Best suited to

Mills with an effluent treatment plant and a freshwater supply that is costly, constrained or under pressure.

Where it stands

Freshwater stream tests in the Essity Valls programme

Concentrate as a resource

Exploring

Our process produces a small, concentrated salt stream rather than a dilute waste. We are working on routes to use it, rather than dispose of it.

What it changes

  • Lower disposal volumes
  • Potential inputs for other industries, such as construction materials or fertiliser

Best suited to

Sites where brine disposal is a cost or a permitting issue.

Where it stands

Assessing options with partners
