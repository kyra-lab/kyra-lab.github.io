Water and vinasse in distilling

For every litre of rum, a distillery produces many litres of vinasse, known as dunder in the Caribbean. It is strong, salty and hard to treat, and many distilleries are on islands where fresh water is precious.

  • Vinasse desalting and potassium recovery
  • Process and cooling water reuse

The challenge

We are looking at how Danu Water can help across the rum-making process, with a particular focus on Caribbean distilleries.

  • Vinasse disposal
    High-strength, high-salt and produced in large volumes, vinasse is difficult and costly to treat or discharge.
  • Limits on recycling dunder
    Many distilleries already return some dunder to fermentation. Salt build-up limits how much they can reuse.
  • Scarce fresh water
    Island distilleries depend on limited supplies for fermentation and cooling.
  • Lost nutrients
    Vinasse is rich in potassium, a valuable fertiliser that is usually wasted.

Progress so far

  • Early-stage work focused on Caribbean rum distilleries
  • Interested in speaking to distilleries willing to share water and vinasse data

2 offerings for distilling. Each status shows how far it has got.

Vinasse desalting and potassium recovery

Exploring

Removal of salts from vinasse, producing a lower-salt stream that is easier to treat or recycle, and a potassium-rich concentrate.

What it changes

  • More dunder can go back to fermentation
  • A fertiliser product instead of a disposal problem
  • Smaller, easier-to-treat effluent

Best suited to

Molasses-based distilleries with vinasse disposal costs or a local need for fertiliser.

Where it stands

Process assessment underway
