Effluent reuse for utilities and cooling

Cooling is one of the largest uses of water in industry, and demand from data centres is rising fast. Much of it is still met with drinking-quality water. Treated municipal effluent is a reliable alternative, once the salts are dealt with.

  • Municipal effluent to cooling water
  • Cooling water for data centres
  • Brackish and saline water

The challenge

Utilities are under pressure to free up supply capacity, while industrial and digital users need large, dependable volumes of cooling water.

  • Competition for potable water
    Every cubic metre of drinking water used for cooling is unavailable for homes and new development.
  • Salts limit cooling cycles
    Hardness and dissolved salts cause scaling and corrosion, forcing frequent blowdown.
  • Effluent is underused
    Treated effluent is a steady, local supply that is usually discharged.
  • By-products need a home
    Treatment trains produce sludges and concentrates. Finding uses for them matters as much as the clean water.

Progress so far

  • Concept design for a staged treatment train from municipal effluent to cooling water make-up
  • Initial market focus on the United States, designed with UK and EU regulations in mind
  • Conversations with data centre and water utility contacts in the US and Japan

What we offer

3 offerings for municipal & utilities. Each status shows how far it has got.

Municipal effluent to cooling water

Feasibility

A treatment train that takes treated municipal effluent and produces cooling water make-up, with Danu Water removing the salts that limit reuse.

What it changes

  • Frees potable supply for homes and growth
  • More cycles of concentration and less blowdown
  • By-products such as lime sludge can go to local farms as agricultural lime

Best suited to

Utilities with industrial or power customers nearby, and users with large cooling demands.

Where it stands

Concept design and water-chemistry modelling

Cooling water for data centres

Exploring

Supplying data centres, including smaller edge sites, with cooling water made from effluent or lower-quality local water instead of mains supply.

What it changes

  • Reduces the water footprint of computing
  • Eases local opposition and permitting
  • A route to water-as-a-service supply

Best suited to

Operators and utilities planning data centre capacity in water-stressed areas.

Where it stands

Discussing pilot opportunities

Brackish and saline water

Exploring

Treatment of wells and other water sources with rising salinity, where reverse osmosis struggles with solids, scaling or recovery.

What it changes

  • High water recovery
  • A concentrate stream that can be used rather than dumped

Best suited to

Communities and businesses whose water source is becoming saltier.
