Adaptive electromembrane separation

A dynamic system that shifts in real time to handle the variability of industrial water.

How it works

Most of what stops water being reused is dissolved: salts such as sodium, chloride and sulphate, hardness from calcium and magnesium, and metals. None of it can be filtered out.

Our system uses an electric field to pull those charged ions out of the water and through selective membranes. One stream comes out with them removed. The other carries them away in concentrated form.

The water itself never has to be pushed through a membrane at high pressure, which is why the approach copes with water that would quickly foul reverse osmosis.

Why adaptive matters

Industrial water changes by the hour: with production rates, products, raw materials, temperature and season. A system tuned for one condition is either over-designed or failing for much of the time.

Ours monitors the incoming water and adjusts how it operates as conditions change. That keeps the output on target and the energy use down, and lets us treat only to the level each use needs.

Our industrial pilot puts this to the test on real, changing paper mill water, at 60 m³ a day on a system built for 400 m³ a day.

  • 95%+ water recoveryfrom industrial wastewater, tuned to each site
  • Low-energy operationno heat and no high-pressure pumping
  • No direct CO₂ emissionsthe system runs on electricity alone
  • Brine reductiona concentrate typically under 10% of the inlet, so disposal energy and costs are dramatically reduced
  • Built for messy watersolids, scaling and variability that defeat other approaches
  • Continuous and unattended24/7 operation, with membranes lasting 5+ years

Complete systems, not just stacks

We design and deliver the whole system around your site: the electromembrane units, any pre-treatment your water needs, and the controls that keep it on target. You tell us what you need the water to do, and we take responsibility for getting it there.

No green premium

Industrial wastewater treatment has long been seen as technically intractable, expensive, or both. Danu Water is designed to pay for itself: through lower freshwater and discharge costs, less downtime from scaling and corrosion, process chemicals that work better when water quality is tuned to what the process needs, and in some cases valuable products recovered from the concentrate. When the business case stands on its own, sustainability follows.
