Adaptive electromembrane separation

A dynamic system that shifts in real time to handle the variability of industrial water.

How it works

Most of what stops wastewater being reused is dissolved salt: sodium, chloride, calcium, sulphate and more. It cannot be filtered out, because it is dissolved.

Our system uses an electric field to pull those charged ions out of the water and through selective membranes. One stream comes out with the salts removed. The other carries them away in concentrated form.

The water itself never has to be pushed through a membrane at high pressure, which is why the approach copes with water that would quickly foul reverse osmosis.

Why adaptive matters

Industrial water changes by the hour: with production rates, products, raw materials, temperature and season. A system tuned for one condition is either over-designed or failing for much of the time.

Ours monitors the incoming water and adjusts how it operates as conditions change. That keeps the output on target and the energy use down, without an operator chasing it.

Our industrial pilots test this directly, running the same streams at fixed and at dynamic setpoints.

No green premium

Industrial wastewater treatment has long been seen as technically intractable, expensive, or both. Danu Water is designed to pay for itself, through lower freshwater and discharge costs, less downtime from scaling and corrosion, and in some cases valuable products recovered from the concentrate. When the business case stands on its own, sustainability follows.
